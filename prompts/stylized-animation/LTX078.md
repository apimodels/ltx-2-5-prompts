## Prompt

````text
Ten seconds, vertical 9:16, stylized 3D animation at feature quality, one continuous shot, ordinary speed. Setting: the rigging shaft running up the inside of a stricken airship at night. Riveted brass ribs, a ladder up one side, gas hoses crossing the space, a tear in the hull at the top of frame with cloud streaming through it. Emergency lamps throwing red. Cast: a stocky badger engineer in oiled canvas overalls, heavy fur, a wrench on a lanyard at his hip. A lean crane bird navigator in a cracked leather flight cap, long legs braced across two ribs. 0 to 3 seconds: the shaft lurches and drops. The badger comes off the ladder, catches a gas hose one-handed and swings out across the shaft. The camera tracks up with him from below. He shouts across: "The forward cell is gone, pull the ballast." 3 to 6 seconds: the bird throws his weight onto a brass release lever with both feet. The lever gives in two stages, the second further than the first. A column of ballast water dumps down past camera and out of frame. His beak stays shut through the whole effort. 6 to 8 seconds: the water clears. The badger lands back on the ladder, hooks an elbow through a rung, and the shaft steadies under them. The bird answers, flat: "That was the last of it." 8 to 10 seconds: both look up at the tear in the hull. Neither speaks. The camera keeps climbing past them toward the cloud coming through, and holds. Mouth rule: one mouth at a time. The badger's jaw moves only on his line and the bird's beak only on his, each holding still while the other talks. Through the final block neither mouth moves. Camera: one continuous climb up the shaft from low to high, accelerating slightly on the water dump and settling at the end. No cuts. Hold across the take: both characters, the overalls, the flight cap, the wrench on its lanyard, the rib spacing and the positions of the red lamps. Sound: two clearly separate voices, the badger low and strained under effort, the bird dry and thin. Structural groaning underneath the whole take, the hose creaking under load, the lever ratcheting in two stages, ballast water roaring past camera and falling away, wind through the tear. Nothing musical. Look: feature-quality stylized 3D, fur and feather detail holding through the motion, soft red emergency light against cold blue coming through the hull tear, real weight in the canvas overalls, water carrying volume. Do not: cut, ramp the speed, add dialogue beyond the two lines above, move both mouths together, or drop the wrench.
````
